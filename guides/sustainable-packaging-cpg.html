Sustainable Packaging for CPG: What Actually Works

Sustainable packaging is not a material choice made in isolation. It is a performance, sourcing, cost, claims, and end-of-life decision that has to survive the supply chain and the shelf.

Jordan Harper, Logic Agency Inc.Updated Sep 202612 min readGuides

Start with the job the package must do

Define the protection, shelf life, shipping, display, opening, and disposal requirements before choosing a material. A package that looks sustainable but fails in transit is not a sustainable system.

Ask what you can prove. Claims need evidence, and retailer or market requirements can change. Keep the specification, supplier documentation, testing, and claim language connected.

The sustainable packaging decision framework

1

Performance

Does the package protect the product through production, freight, storage, fulfillment, and use?

2

Material

What is it made from, how much of it is used, and what alternatives are genuinely available at your volume?

3

Economics

Model tooling, minimums, conversion, freight, damage, assembly, and disposal—not only unit price.

4

Claims

Define the evidence behind recycled, recyclable, compostable, renewable, or reduced-material claims.

5

Retail fit

Confirm case pack, palletization, shelf presentation, labeling, and retailer requirements before you lock the design.

6

End of life

Consider what a customer can realistically do with the package in the markets where it is sold.

Make the supplier and design decisions together

A material decision is only real when a qualified supplier can produce it at the required quality, volume, and timing. Ask for samples, test the package in the actual use case, and understand the supplier’s minimums and lead times.

Bring sourcing into the design process early. The cheapest time to discover a material or tooling constraint is before the design is final.

What to measure after launch

Track damage, returns, material usage, package weight, cost variance, production yield, customer feedback, and claim performance. Sustainable packaging is a system you improve—not a badge you print once.

Be specific about tradeoffs. A smaller material reduction that works reliably may be better than a dramatic material switch that creates waste elsewhere.

Operator rule. Do not promise what the package cannot prove, produce, or survive.

Frequently Asked Questions

Is recyclable packaging always more sustainable?

Not necessarily. The answer depends on material, local recovery systems, contamination, production inputs, transport, reuse, and actual consumer behavior. Evaluate the full system.

How can sustainable packaging reduce cost?

Material reduction, right-sized formats, lower damage, better case packs, and fewer components can reduce cost. Sustainable changes can also increase tooling or conversion cost, so model both.

When should sourcing be involved in packaging design?

Early. Supplier capability, minimums, tooling, lead time, testing, and available materials can materially change the design and the business case.
